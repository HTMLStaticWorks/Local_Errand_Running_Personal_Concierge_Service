ETHICAL FRAMEWORK

The 5 Core Principles of Equal Worth

Time banking is more than swapping chores—it is a proven social model developed to value human dignity and rebuild genuine community connection.

1. We Are All Assets

Every human being has meaningful contributions to make. Whether you offer technical support, cook meals, drive a neighbor, or provide friendly conversation, your time has vital worth.

2. Redefining Real Work

Market economies overlook unpaid care, elder support, and neighborliness. Time banking formally recognizes, validates, and rewards these essential social foundations.

3. Reciprocity Beats Charity

One-way charity can create dependency and unease. Two-way reciprocity transforms passive recipients into valued givers, establishing equal standing and mutual respect.

4. Rebuilding Social Capital

Strong neighborhoods are built on trust and shared networks. By connecting neighbors directly, time banking weaves a resilient safety net that protects communities in times of crisis.

5. Respect & Equal Parity

An hour is an hour. The hour an attorney spends giving legal guidance carries the exact same time credit as the hour a retiree spends weeding a garden or delivering groceries.

Traditional markets assign dollar values based on scarcity and financial leverage, often undervaluing vital care, companionship, and everyday assistance. In time banking, everyone’s fundamental human time is treated as having equal intrinsic dignity. One hour spent helping a neighbor with groceries has the exact same value as one hour spent setting up a computer. This fundamental parity removes socioeconomic barriers and makes everyone an equal partner.

In the United States and most jurisdictions, reciprocal community time banking exchanges are explicitly recognized as non-commercial, non-taxable neighborly mutual aid (supported by IRS Private Letter Ruling 97-22002). Because credits cannot be redeemed for cash or traded for market goods, they are non-monetary and not subject to income tax.
